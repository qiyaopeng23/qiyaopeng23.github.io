About

A note on my work

Welcome. I'm Qiyao Peng, a Ph.D. candidate in the STEM-designated Communication program at the University of California, Santa Barbara, expected to graduate in June 2027.

My research sits at the intersection of health communication, persuasion, and computational social science. Using experimental, computational, and neurophysiological methods (fMRI, eye-tracking), I study how messages — from public-service announcements to AI-generated content — shape attitudes, beliefs, and behaviors related to health and well-being.

I work with Dr. Jiaying Liu in the CHARM Lab at UCSB, the Computational Multimodal Communication Lab with Prof. Cuihua Shen, Dr. Yilang Peng, and Dr. Yingdan Lu, and the COMAP Lab at Northwestern University. Before academia, I led brand and marketing for a Beijing-based financial-IT firm and earned my M.A. from USC Annenberg.

When I'm not running studies, I'm probably learning a new language (currently five at varying degrees of rust), watching films, or writing about media and emotion.

Research

Three threads of inquiry

My work moves between message design, audience psychology, and the computational tools that let us study both at scale. Three threads run through it:

01 / Health

Persuasive Health Messages

Designing and testing anti-vaping, vaccine, and prevention messages using fMRI, eye-tracking, and machine-learning analyses of message profiles.

02 / AI

Generative AI & Misinformation

The visual realism, misinformation potential, and surrealist signatures of photorealistic AI-generated images across platforms.

03 / Media

News, Norms, & Engagement

How news headlines, user comments, and emotional framing shape norm perceptions and engagement in digital media environments.
